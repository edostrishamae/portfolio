About

From civil engineering to executive operations.

I found I enjoyed applying that thinking to businesses more than to structures, building the spreadsheet, the tracker, or the automation that quietly keeps an operation from falling behind.

Project Overview

A full inventory and business intelligence system built in Google Sheets and Google Apps Script for LXTR Perfume, a fragrance retail business. What started as a stock tracker has grown into an executive dashboard with 15 live KPIs, automatic reorder logic, and a suite of Apps Script automations (low-stock alerts, one-click PDF invoices, and one-click backups), so the owner runs the business from one workbook instead of stitching together spreadsheets, notes, and memory.

Business Problem

Retail inventory is easy to lose track of once a catalog grows past a handful of products. Without a system, stock counts, reorder points, and profit margins live in someone's memory or in disconnected notes, which means stockouts, overstock, and no clear read on which products are actually worth reordering, let alone when to reorder them or how much.

Solution

I designed a single source of truth: a Product Master sheet holding SKU-level cost, price, and lead-time data, feeding into Purchases and Sales logs (the only two sheets requiring manual entry), which in turn drive a fully automatic Inventory sheet, a Reports sheet, and a 15-KPI Executive Dashboard. On top of that sits an Apps Script layer that handles order numbering, validation, alerts, invoicing, and backups, so the system runs itself between data entries.

Workflow

01
Product Master as the source of truth
Every product is defined once: cost, retail price, minimum stock level, and lead time, with duplicate-SKU blocking so downstream calculations never inherit a bad entry.
02
Purchases and Sales are the only manual entry
Logging a purchase or sale is the one hands-on step left. Order numbers auto-generate (ORD-0001, etc.) the moment a SKU is entered, and built-in validation blocks invalid dates, negative quantities, and duplicates.
03
Inventory recalculates itself, with judgment built in
Stock levels, status (OK / Low / Out), Safety Stock, Reorder Point, and Suggested Restock Qty all update automatically, alongside ABC Classification and Movement Status, which flag which products actually drive revenue.
04
The Executive Dashboard surfaces 15 KPIs and 3 live charts
Inventory value, revenue, profit, margin, turnover, and an overall inventory health score sit alongside auto-updating monthly trend, best-seller, and stock-status charts.
05
Apps Script closes the loop
A custom "LXTR Tools" menu sends low-stock and daily summary emails, generates a PDF invoice per sale with one click, and backs up the whole workbook to Drive on demand.

Tools Used

Google Sheets
Google Apps Script
SUMIFS / conditional formulas
Data validation
Conditional formatting

AI-assisted system design

Automation Logic

Stock levels are calculated by netting logged purchases against sales per SKU. Reorder logic goes a step further: Safety Stock and Reorder Point are calculated from each product's sales velocity and lead time, and a Suggested Restock Qty is generated automatically, turning "what's low" into "what to order and roughly how much." ABC Classification and a Performance Score rank products by actual revenue contribution, and every formula cell is locked so only real input cells (Product Master, Purchases, Sales) can be edited, protecting the logic underneath.

Key Features

  • 15-KPI Executive Dashboard: inventory value/cost, revenue, profit, margin, stock counts, monthly revenue/profit, average order value, inventory turnover, and an overall inventory health score
  • 3 auto-updating charts: monthly revenue & profit trend, top 5 best-sellers, and stock status breakdown
  • Smart inventory fields: Safety Stock, Reorder Point, Suggested Restock Qty, ABC Classification, Movement Status, and Performance Score
  • Auto-generated Order Numbers and built-in validation (no invalid dates, negative quantities, or duplicate SKUs/orders) on every logged purchase or sale
  • Custom "LXTR Tools" Apps Script menu: low-stock and daily summary emails, one-click PDF invoice generation, one-click Drive backup
  • Locked formula cells and consistent dashboard styling, so the system is safe and readable for someone other than its builder
Business Impact: The business owner can check one dashboard and know, without opening a single log sheet, what's low, what's selling, what to reorder and roughly how much, and what the business is actually worth right now, turning inventory management from a periodic manual audit into a live, always-current, self-protecting system.

Skills Demonstrated

  • Systems design (separating data entry from reporting)
  • Formula-based and script-based automation
  • Inventory planning logic (safety stock, reorder point, ABC analysis)
  • Google Apps Script (triggers, email automation, PDF generation)
  • Dashboard design for non-technical end users

Lessons Learned

The hardest part wasn't the formulas. It was deciding what the dashboard should not show, and then, later, what to automate versus what to leave as a deliberate manual step. Locking formula cells and keeping Purchases/Sales as the only manual entry points came from watching where accidental edits could actually break something. The system is still evolving: monthly trend charts and time-based KPIs sit at zero until real sale dates are logged, which is the next real-data milestone.

Project Overview

A self-initiated CRM analytics dashboard built in Google Sheets to demonstrate sales-operations reporting — turning lead and deal activity into a live, filterable view of business performance across 99 leads and 65 closed deals, using a realistic multi-year, multi-product dataset I built to model how a real sales pipeline behaves.

Business Problem

Sales data is only useful if someone can answer questions about it quickly: which month was strongest, which product is actually driving revenue, where leads are dropping out of the funnel. Without a structured dashboard, those answers require manually filtering a raw spreadsheet every time a question comes up.

Solution

I built a dashboard with dropdown-driven filters (Year, Month, Sales Rep, Product, Sales Status) sitting on top of separate Leads and Sales data sheets. Changing a dropdown instantly recalculates filtered revenue, deal count, and average deal size, so anyone can answer a specific question without touching a single formula.

Workflow

01
Raw activity captured separately
Leads and Sales are logged on their own sheets, keeping raw data entry independent from reporting logic.
02
A Helpers sheet does the heavy lifting
Intermediate calculations (monthly totals, product rollups, funnel stage counts) are computed once, off to the side, so the dashboard stays fast and readable.
03
Filters recompute the top-line KPIs
Selecting a Year, Sales Rep, Product, or Status recalculates Filtered Revenue, Filtered Deals, and Filtered Average Deal instantly.
04
Trends render automatically
Monthly and yearly revenue, a revenue heatmap by month, and top-product rankings are all chart-fed from the same underlying data, with no manual chart rebuilding.
05
The funnel tells its own story
Lead → Contacted → Qualified → Proposal → Won is tracked stage by stage, so drop-off is visible at a glance instead of buried in raw records.

Tools Used

Google Sheets
Data validation dropdowns
SUMIFS / COUNTIFS logic
Native charting
Helper-sheet architecture

Automation Logic

KPI cards, including total leads, total deals, converted leads, conversion rate, average order value, average revenue per lead, revenue this month and this year, highest sales month, best-performing product, and best sales day, are all formula-driven off the Leads and Sales sheets, so they update the moment new activity is logged. The conversion funnel and product rankings recompute the same way, with no manual pivot-table rebuilding required.

Key Features

  • Instant-filter dashboard (Year / Month / Sales Rep / Product / Sales Status)
  • Full KPI suite: revenue, deals, conversion rate, average deal size, best month, best product, best sales day
  • Five-stage conversion funnel with stage-by-stage lead counts
  • Revenue heatmap by month and year, plus a ranked product-performance table
  • Sales-status breakdown (Closed Won, Closed Lost, In Negotiation, Refunded)
Business Impact: Anyone reviewing the business, not just the person who built the sheet, can filter to a specific rep, product, or period and get a trustworthy answer in seconds, turning "let me pull that together" into "it's already there."

Skills Demonstrated

  • Dashboard architecture (separating raw data, helper logic, and presentation)
  • KPI design for non-technical stakeholders
  • Funnel and conversion analysis
  • Sales operations reporting

Lessons Learned

A dashboard that's technically correct but slow to read is still a bad dashboard. Splitting calculations into a dedicated Helpers sheet kept every formula simpler and the actual dashboard sheet fast to scan, a small architectural decision that made the whole system more maintainable.

Project Overview

A self-initiated tracker built in Google Sheets and Google Apps Script to demonstrate vendor and subscription management — organizing every software tool a business might rely on across 20 categories, from CRM and Advertising to AI Tools and Development, with automatic email reminders ahead of renewal dates.

Business Problem

Growing businesses accumulate software fast, a CRM here, an automation tool there, and renewal dates are easy to lose track of across dozens of vendors. Missed cancellations lead to paying for tools nobody uses; missed renewals lead to a tool shutting off mid-project. Both are avoidable, and both usually come down to nobody having one place to check.

Solution

I built a Lists tab defining a controlled vocabulary: 20 tool categories, 8 status states (Active, Trial, Needs Review, Expired, Cancelled, Not In Use, Pending Setup, Archived), subscription types, and billing frequencies, feeding standardized dropdowns into a central Tool Tracker sheet, plus a Google Apps Script that emails a reminder 14 days before each renewal date.

Workflow

01
Define the vocabulary once
Categories, statuses, subscription types, and billing frequencies are defined on a dedicated Lists tab, so every entry uses the same language instead of free-text notes.
02
Log every tool consistently
Each subscription is tagged by category, status, type, and billing frequency through dropdowns pulled from the Lists tab, keeping data entry fast and consistent.
03
Status color-coding surfaces risk at a glance
A status legend (Active, Trial, Expired, Cancelled, and so on) is color-coded, so a scan of the tracker shows what needs attention without reading every row.
04
A script watches renewal dates
Google Apps Script checks upcoming renewal dates and automatically sends a reminder email 14 days ahead, so renewal decisions happen on purpose, not by default.
05
Retired tools move to Archive
A dedicated Archive tab keeps discontinued subscriptions out of the active tracker without deleting the history.

Tools Used

Google Sheets
Google Apps Script
Data validation
Conditional formatting
Time-based triggers

Automation Logic

The Apps Script layer is what turns this from a tracker into a system: rather than someone needing to remember to check renewal dates, the script compares each subscription's renewal date against today's date and fires an email automatically once a tool is 14 days out from renewal, removing the manual date-checking step entirely.

Key Features

  • Standardized taxonomy across 20 tool categories and 8 status states
  • Color-coded status legend for at-a-glance review
  • Automatic email reminders 14 days ahead of subscription renewal
  • Separate Dashboard, Archive, and Instructions tabs, so the system is usable by someone other than its builder
Business Impact: Software spend stops being something the business finds out about after the fact. Renewal decisions (keep, downgrade, or cancel) get made intentionally, ahead of the charge, instead of by default.

Skills Demonstrated

  • Taxonomy and data-standardization design
  • Google Apps Script automation
  • Vendor and subscription management
  • System documentation for handoff

Lessons Learned

A tracker is only as good as the categories behind it. Building the Lists tab first, before a single tool was logged, meant every entry afterward was consistent, which is what made the automation reliable. Structure has to come before automation, not after.

Project Overview

I designed and built an executive inbox operations system that turns a busy Gmail inbox into a structured, prioritized workflow. The system evaluates incoming Gmail messages against multiple operational signals, applies structured Gmail labels, identifies messages that need a reply, prepares reply drafts where appropriate, tracks follow-ups, and logs activity into Google Sheets. A companion web dashboard presents the operational picture at a glance: priority levels, pending replies, follow-ups, meetings, and inbox trends, built to expose operational metadata rather than email content.

Business Problem

A busy executive inbox can quietly become a second task list. Important messages compete with newsletters, automated notifications, routine communication, meeting requests, and items that need a follow-up, and manually reviewing all of it, deciding what deserves attention, and remembering what still needs a reply creates unnecessary administrative overhead. The goal was a workflow that could handle the first layer of organization automatically, while keeping the final communication decision with the human.

Solution

I built a Gmail + Google Apps Script + Google Sheets workflow with a live web dashboard on top. Incoming messages are evaluated against multiple signals, scored for priority, and organized with structured Gmail labels. Messages that need a reply are flagged, qualifying messages receive a prepared draft that is never sent automatically, and follow-up requirements are tracked. Everything is logged as structured metadata in Google Sheets, which feeds a dashboard built to show inbox activity without exposing email content.

Workflow

01
Incoming email arrives
Gmail receives the message and a scheduled Apps Script trigger picks it up for processing.
02
Multi-signal classification
VIP status, urgency, deadlines, meetings, direct questions, response requirements, accounting, travel, team communication, and newsletter indicators are all evaluated together.
03
Priority scoring
The signals combine into a priority score, resolved into Critical, High, Medium, or Low, never a single-keyword decision.
04
Automated Gmail labeling
Structured labels (VIP Clients, Urgent, Pending Reply, Follow-Up, Meetings, Accounting, Travels, Team, Personal, Spam/Newsletter) are applied automatically based on classification.
05
Reply preparation & follow-up tracking
Qualifying messages receive a prepared reply draft, never sent automatically, while messages needing further attention are flagged and tracked for follow-up.
06
Structured logging & dashboard
Operational metadata is logged to Google Sheets and surfaced on a live executive dashboard showing priorities, pending replies, meetings, and trends.

Tools Used

Automation Logic

The priority engine doesn't rely on a single keyword. It combines multiple signals: VIP status, urgent language, deadlines, direct questions, meetings, client or team communication, and response requirements, into a single priority score. That classification then drives Gmail organization, surfaces the messages that need attention first, flags follow-up requirements, and, where appropriate, prepares a reply draft. The workflow is built to support executive decision-making, not replace it.

Key Features

  • Gmail inbox automation running on scheduled Apps Script triggers
  • Multi-signal priority scoring across Critical, High, Medium, and Low levels
  • Automated Gmail labeling (VIP, Urgent, Pending Reply, Follow-Up, Meetings, Accounting, Travel, Team, Personal, Spam/Newsletter)
  • Reply draft preparation that always requires human review before sending
  • Follow-up tracking and structured activity logging in Google Sheets
  • Live executive dashboard built on structured, privacy-conscious metadata
Human-in-the-Loop by Design: Automation handles the repetitive first layer of inbox organization, while the human remains in control of final communication. Reply drafts are prepared for review. They are never sent automatically.
Business Impact: The system reduces repetitive inbox triage, creates a structured priority layer, and makes follow-up requirements easier to surface across different categories of communication. Inbox activity is centralized into structured reporting, giving a faster read on what actually needs attention, while the final communication decision stays with the human.

Skills Demonstrated

  • Executive inbox management and operations
  • Workflow automation and process design
  • Google Apps Script and Gmail automation
  • Priority logic and data organization
  • Dashboard architecture and operational reporting
  • AI-assisted, human-in-the-loop system design
  • Privacy-conscious system design

Lessons Learned

Effective automation isn't about making everything autonomous. For executive communication specifically, classification, organization, prioritization, and draft preparation can all be automated while the final communication decision stays with the human. Building this reinforced how much of good automation comes down to clear operational rules, structured data, and designing for oversight, not just writing the script.

My Process

The same process, four times over.

Every system in this portfolio was built the same way. The approach doesn't change; only the operational problem does.

01

Diagnose the manual process

Start by understanding exactly how the work is currently being done by hand: what's tracked, what's missed, and where the bottleneck actually lives.

02

Map the real workflow

Trace the process from first input to final decision, so the system mirrors how the business actually operates, not an idealized version of it.

03

Design the structure first

Define categories, statuses, and data relationships before building a single formula. Structure is what makes automation reliable later.

04

Automate the repetitive parts

Use formulas, scripts, and AI-assisted logic to remove the steps a person shouldn't have to do by hand: calculations, flags, reminders.

05

Test against real data

Run the system against actual numbers, not sample data, to confirm it holds up under real, messy conditions.

06

Document for handoff

Write the system down clearly enough that someone else can maintain it, a habit carried straight over from engineering documentation.
